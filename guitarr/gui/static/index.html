Calibrate latency

Exact: with a cable (nobody plays)

Connect a cable from the output where the metronome sounds into a free input of your interface. Turn off direct monitoring and keep that input's gain low (a loop can whistle). It measures the system alone.

How to connect the cable, step by step

What you need

An instrument cable (6.3 mm plug on both ends, like your guitar's). If the metronome plays from your computer's headphone jack, a 3.5 mm to 6.3 mm cable.

Steps

  1. In Metronome output (above), choose your interface. Then the cable goes from the interface back into itself.
  2. Turn direct monitoring off (the MONITOR / DIRECT button or knob) and take your headphones off: with the cable in, a loop can whistle loudly.
  3. Turn the gain of input 2 all the way down, and the headphone volume to about half.
  4. Plug the cable from the interface's headphone output (or a line output) into input 2. Leave the guitar in input 1, with its volume knob down.
  5. In Input with the cable choose 2 and press Measure with the cable. 13 clicks go out and come back; don't play or touch anything for about 8 seconds.
  6. When it says ✓, unplug the cable, turn the monitoring back on if you use it, and do Playing along with the guitar: that one tells you your own habit.

If it doesn't work

  • “The clicks didn't come back”: the cable is in another input than the one chosen, the metronome plays on another device (the speakers?), the headphone volume is at zero, or the gain is too low (raise it a little at a time).
  • “Irregular clicks”: direct monitoring is still on (the click goes round and round), the gain is so high it distorts, or the guitar was sounding.
  • Only one input? Unplug the guitar, use input 1 for the cable and choose 1.
  • Metronome on the computer? It also works: a cable from the computer's headphone jack into the interface. It measures that output, which is the one you hear.

Playing along

You'll hear 4 clicks to catch the pulse and then 12 more. Hit the muted strings (a short pick stroke) right on every click. Use headphones.

Tips

  • Your level should be between −30 and −10 dB while playing. If it turns red, lower the gain.
  • A clean signal (no distortion) works best; monitor your effects separately.
  • If soft attacks aren't detected, raise the sensitivity or lower the noise gate.
